# ICE Operations Brief — Content Reference

## The Amazon Model

Acting ICE Director Todd Lyons outlined plans to transform the deportation process into an industrial-scale operation modeled after efficiency benchmarks from major logistics companies. Internal ICE documents detail a draft infrastructure plan to convert industrial warehouses into detention centers capable of holding over 80,000 people simultaneously. The system architecture relies on 16 smaller "feeder" sites that funnel detainees into seven massive regional processing hubs.

### Proposed Major Facility Locations

- **Stafford, Virginia** — capacity up to 10,000 beds
- **Hutchins, Texas** — capacity up to 9,500 beds
- **Hammond, Indiana** — capacity up to 9,000 beds
- Additional facilities planned in Arizona, Georgia, and Missouri

### Financial Scale

- **Projected total cost:** $38 billion
- **Congressional allocation:** $45 billion for immigrant detention (the largest amount ever appropriated)
- **Design contract:** ICE signed a $29.9 million "concept design" contract for processing centers and mega-facilities

### Strategic Placement

Warehouses are strategically located 45 minutes to 1 hour outside major metropolitan areas, positioned near major logistics hubs and transportation corridors to facilitate the rapid movement of detainees through the system.

**Sources:** Bloomberg, Colorado Newsline, PBS, NBC News, Truthout, American Immigration Council, WTOP

## The Killing of Renée Good (January 7, 2026)

**Victim:** Renée Good, 37-year-old American citizen, Minneapolis, Minnesota

### Incident Details

- Good was in her vehicle, stopped sideways across a street
- An ICE agent, Jonathan Ross, circled the vehicle and ordered her to exit
- Ross reached through the window toward Good
- Good briefly reversed her vehicle, then moved forward and to the right, in the direction of traffic
- Agent Ross was positioned at the front-left of the vehicle
- Ross fired 3 shots as the vehicle passed him, with the car moving **away** from the agent's position

### Official Claims vs. Evidence

- **DHS claim:** Good was "weaponizing her SUV to run over an ICE agent"
- **Video evidence:** Multiple analyses show the vehicle was moving away from the agent, contradicting the claim she posed an immediate threat

### Public Response

**Minneapolis Mayor Jacob Frey** (after reviewing video evidence): "Having seen the video myself, I want to tell everybody directly that is bullshit" and "To ICE, get the fuck out of Minneapolis"

**Video Sources:** CNN analysis, Washington Post investigation, ABC News timeline

## The Killing of Alex Pretti (January 24, 2026)

**Victim:** Alex Pretti, 37-year-old Virginia intensive care nurse, Minneapolis, Minnesota

### Incident Details

- Killed by two Customs and Border Protection (CBP) officers on Nicollet Avenue in Minneapolis
- Pretti was filming law enforcement with his phone and directing traffic
- He positioned himself between a CBP agent and a woman who had been thrown to the ground
- Pretti placed his arm protectively around the woman
- He was pepper-sprayed and wrestled to the ground by multiple agents
- Video evidence demonstrates his firearm was **secured by agents before lethal force was deployed**
- One agent was recorded emerging from the physical struggle already holding Pretti's firearm
- **10 shots were fired in under 5 seconds** (confirmed by forensic audio analysis)

### Official Claims vs. Evidence

- **DHS claim:** Pretti "approached officers with a 9mm semi-automatic handgun" and "violently resisted"
- **Video evidence:** Multiple independent forensic analyses from Bellingcat, CNN, Washington Post, NPR, and ABC News directly contradict all DHS claims about the sequence of events

### Community Response

- Thousands gathered for vigils the same evening
- **Tens of thousands participated in demonstrations the following day**

**Video Sources:** Bellingcat, CNN visual analysis, Washington Post, NPR, ABC News

## The Detention of Liam Conejo Ramos

**Subject:** Liam Conejo Ramos, 5-year-old boy, Columbia Heights, Minnesota

### Timeline of Events

- **January 20, 2026:** Liam and his father Adrian were detained on a suburban Minneapolis driveway while the boy was walking home from school
- **Transport:** Sent 1,300 miles from Minnesota to Dilley Immigration Processing Center in Texas
- **Legal status:** The family had entered the United States legally in December 2024, presented themselves to border officials, and had an active asylum claim pending
- **Detention basis:** No order of deportation existed at the time of detention

### Legal Action

- **January 31, 2026:** Federal judge ordered Liam's release
- **Following release:** DHS filed for expedited deportation
- **Assessment by family's legal counsel:** The expedited deportation filing was characterized as "retaliatory"

### Media Documentation

A widely circulated photograph captured Liam in a plaid coat and blue knit bunny hat, surrounded by agents. The image circulated across mainstream media outlets and has become emblematic of the detention operation's impact on children.

**Photo availability:** AP, Reuters, CNN (check outlets for licensing information)
