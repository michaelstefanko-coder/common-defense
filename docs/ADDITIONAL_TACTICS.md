# Additional Resistance Tactics

This document outlines expanded nonviolent resistance strategies for the Common Defense platform, beyond the original five core campaigns. Each tactic is designed with sufficient detail for developers to build campaign pages, track participation, and measure impact.

## Economic Pressure Tactics

**Rationale:** Amazon Web Services provides ICE's entire digital backbone — data storage, surveillance infrastructure, AI/ML processing. Amazon's logistics expertise is explicitly sought by ICE leadership for scaling operations.

**Campaign Focus:** While pressuring AWS directly, use Whole Foods as the visible consumer-facing pressure point.

**Actions:**
- Coordinated "Cancel Prime" awareness days with sign-ups and confirmations
- Whole Foods picket coordination and consumer education
- Transparency demands for AWS contracts with DHS/ICE
- Social media campaigns featuring Amazon executive statements on immigration

**Campaign Page Should Include:**
- Prime cancellation verification tracker
- AWS-to-ICE connection map (showing data services)
- Whole Foods location finder for local organizing
- Letter templates to Amazon shareholders
- Progress metrics: Prime cancellations reported, pledge tracker

**Impact Metrics:**
- Prime subscription cancellations
- Whole Foods foot traffic reduction (where data available)
- Media coverage of boycott growth
- Amazon shareholder pressure indicators

**Legal Status:** Consumer boycotts are fully protected First Amendment activity.

**Difficulty Level:** Easy to Medium
**Time Commitment:** 5 minutes (cancellation) to ongoing (organizing)
**Legal Risk:** None
**Impact Potential:** High

### 2. Bank Divestment Campaign

**Rationale:** Modeled on 2011 Bank Transfer Day and apartheid-era divestment. Mass account closures create immediate pressure on bank leadership and visible impact on quarterly earnings reports.

**Campaign Focus:** Identify banks providing financing to ICE contractors, detention facility operators, and surveillance companies. Coordinate mass account closures on a single designated day.

**Research Phase:**
- Comprehensive research identifying banks by:
  - Loans to private detention operators (CoreCivic, GEO Group)
  - Investments in surveillance companies (Palantir, Clearview AI)
  - Bonds and securities for ICE-related infrastructure
  - Financing for law enforcement agencies

**Campaign Actions:**
- Public research publication of bank relationships
- Education phase: webinars on private detention financing
- Coordinated move-your-money day with bank walk-outs
- Direct transfer to credit unions and community banks

**Campaign Page Should Include:**
- Bank tracker showing connections to detention/surveillance
- Credit union locator by region
- Step-by-step account closure guides
- Letter templates to bank executives
- Real-time counter of accounts moved
- Before/after comparison of total deposits moved

**Historical Precedent:**
- 2011 Bank Transfer Day: 650,000+ accounts moved in one month
- Apartheid divestment campaigns: reduced bank financing for South African government
- Tobacco divestment: institutional investors divested $100+ billion

**Primary Targets:**
- JPMorgan Chase (largest US bank, historically tied to private prisons)
- Wells Fargo (extensive mortgage lending to detention facilities)
- Bank of America (securities underwriting for detention bonds)

**Impact Metrics:**
- Accounts moved/deposits transferred
- Estimated dollar amounts moved
- Participating credit unions and community banks
- Media pickup of divestment numbers
- Bank stock price monitoring during campaign

**Legal Status:** Completely legal. Banks cannot penalize account closure.

**Difficulty Level:** Easy
**Time Commitment:** 30 minutes to 2 hours (account transfer)
**Legal Risk:** None
**Impact Potential:** High

## Digital Resistance Tactics

### 4. Algorithm Flooding (Ad-Cost Inflation) — Advanced Strategies

**Rationale:** Government recruitment and propaganda ads have budget constraints. Coordinated interaction drives up cost-per-click, forcing agencies to choose between higher spending or reduced reach.

**Basic Actions (Already in Platform):**
- Manual clicking of visible ICE/DHS recruitment ads in user feeds
- Shares and engagement that trigger ad spending

**Advanced Coordination:**
- Scheduled "flooding windows" — coordinated timing across all participants
- Target specific high-visibility placements (Facebook, Instagram, YouTube recruitment campaigns)
- Coordinate during budget reporting periods for maximum visibility in agency financials

**Campaign Page Should Include:**
- Real-time counter of ad interactions
- Tutorial: identifying and clicking ICE/DHS ads safely
- Leaderboard: participation by region
- Cost calculation: estimated budget impact
- News feed: tracking agency ad spending announcements
- Browser extension option (see Legal Note below)

**Legal / Technical Notes:**
- **LEGAL:** Clicking ads in your feed that you see naturally is protected activity
- **ILLEGAL:** Automated click bots, script-driven interactions, or third-party traffic = wire fraud
- **Safe:** Manual, coordinated clicking by real users viewing real ads
- **Extension Option:** Browser extension that highlights and surfaces ICE/DHS ads (user still clicks manually)

**Impact Metrics:**
- Total ad interactions tracked
- Estimated cost-per-click increases
- Agency ad spend changes (monitored through public filing analysis)
- Campaign reach across platforms

**Difficulty Level:** Easy
**Time Commitment:** 5-15 minutes daily (optional)
**Legal Risk:** None (if manual/coordinated only)
**Impact Potential:** Medium

### 6. Data Broker Poisoning

**Rationale:** Intelligence agencies and law enforcement rely on data broker aggregation. Degrading data quality reduces the operational value of surveillance databases like Palantir.

**Campaign Focus:** Two-track approach — Legal Opting Out + Strategic False Information

**Track A: Legal Data Broker Opt-Out (Clear Legal Status)**
- Comprehensive guide to opting out of major data brokers
- Automated opt-out submission tools
- Regular re-verification (brokers often re-add data)
- Documentation of opt-out processes

**Data Brokers to Target:**
- People search sites: BeenVerified, Spokeo, ZoomInfo
- Data aggregators: Acxiom, Experian, Equifax
- Location data brokers: X-Mode, Locateplus
- Behavioral data: Oracle Data Cloud, Epsilon
- Law enforcement tools: Clearview AI, LexisNexis threat screening

**Track B: Strategic False Information (Legal Gray Area)**
- Submission of deliberately conflicting personal information
- Use of legal name variations to fragments records
- PO boxes instead of residential addresses
- Privacy service mail forwarding
- Timing strategies to confuse database reconciliation

**Legal Considerations:**
- **Clearly Legal:** Opting out, using legal name variations, PO boxes
- **Gray Area:** Submitting false information about yourself (jurisdiction-dependent; may violate wire fraud statutes in some contexts)
- **Safer Alternative:** Focus on opting out and privacy hardening rather than false data

**Campaign Page Should Include:**
- Opt-out tracker: data brokers and opt-out status
- Automated opt-out tools (where available)
- Guide to legal name variations and privacy services
- PO box setup guide
- Regular re-verification calendar
- VPN/privacy tool recommendations
- Credit freeze and identity monitoring resources
- Leaderboard: participation by region
- Legal analysis: jurisdiction-specific guidance

**Impact Metrics:**
- Successful opt-outs documented
- Re-appearance rate (how often brokers re-add data)
- Overall data broker coverage reduction
- Media coverage of data broker poisoning strategy

**Difficulty Level:** Easy to Medium
**Time Commitment:** 30 minutes per broker
**Legal Risk:** Low (opt-out); Consult attorney (false information strategy)
**Impact Potential:** Medium

**Function 1: ICE Activity Monitoring**
- Document ICE agent activity at known offices and staging areas
- Photo and video documentation (First Amendment protected)
- Record date, time, location, number of agents, vehicles
- Share real-time alerts through encrypted Signal/WhatsApp chains

**Function 2: Know Your Rights Education**
- Train community members on rights during ICE encounter
- Wallet-sized rights cards in multiple languages
- Video guides (5-10 minutes) in 10+ languages
- Training sessions at community centers, churches, organizations

**Function 3: Rapid Legal Response**
- Immediate connection to legal support for detained individuals
- Coordination with immigration attorneys
- Family notification support
- Bond/bail coordination

**Function 4: Community Alert Network**
- Real-time alerts of ICE activity via encrypted messaging
- Rapid response to help people avoid enforcement areas
- Support for individuals who have been detained

**Campaign Page Should Include:**
- Observer training modules (online and in-person)
- Know-your-rights materials (multi-language):
  - Printable wallet cards
  - QR codes linking to video guides
  - Posters for businesses/organizations
- Encrypted messaging setup guides
- Legal resources and attorney network
- Documentation standards and templates
- Safety protocols for observers
- Incident reporting system
- Real-time alert map (optional; privacy considerations)
- Leaderboard: trained observers by community

**Legal Protections:**
- First Amendment right to photograph/film police in public
- Know-your-rights education is protected speech
- Observers should not interfere with enforcement (observation only)
- Recording with consent in two-party consent states

**Difficulty Level:** Medium
**Time Commitment:** 2-4 hours training; 1-2 hours observation/month
**Legal Risk:** Low (if protocols followed)
**Impact Potential:** Medium to High

### 9. ICE-Free Zone Campaigns

**Rationale:** Municipal ordinances limiting or prohibiting immigration enforcement on city property create legal frameworks protecting residents and limiting ICE operational reach.

**Campaign Focus:** Support municipal governments adopting ICE-Free Zone ordinances.

**Policy Components:**

**Component 1: Property Restrictions**
- Prohibit use of city property for immigration enforcement
- ICE cannot conduct operations from city facilities
- City cannot provide resources (utilities, security) to ICE

**Component 2: Employee Restrictions**
- City employees cannot cooperate with ICE civil immigration inquiries
- Exception: response to criminal warrants from judges
- Immigration detainers (without judicial warrant) are non-binding

**Component 3: Warrant Requirements**
- ICE detainers without judicial warrants are not enforceable
- City will not honor detainers not backed by probable cause warrants
- Judicial review required for all immigration holds

**Component 4: Legal Authority**
- Model: San Francisco's approach
- City attorney authority to take legal action against unlawful ICE use
- Precedent: Ninth Circuit upholds municipal authority to restrict local cooperation

**Campaign Actions:**
- Identify target municipalities (start with sympathetic local governments)
- Research current ICE presence and operations
- Build community coalition with immigration organizations
- Develop local ordinance tailored to jurisdiction
- Public education and media campaign
- City council presentation and testimony
- Build political pressure (constituent calls, council member meetings)

**Campaign Page Should Include:**
- Template ordinances (customizable by jurisdiction)
- Legal analysis and precedent cases
- Step-by-step guide for organizing at local level
- Talking points and fact sheets
- Sample city council testimony
- Media coverage of successful ordinances
- Map of ICE-Free Zones (as adopted)
- Coalition organization toolkit
- Impact tracking: ordinances adopted, people protected

**Successful Jurisdictions:**
- San Francisco (2018+)
- Oakland (2018+)
- Los Angeles (2017+)
- Austin (2021+)
- Cook County/Chicago area (2020+)

**Impact Metrics:**
- Ordinances adopted
- Jurisdictions covered
- Estimated population protected
- ICE presence/operations changes
- Legal cases challenging or defending ordinances

**Difficulty Level:** Advanced
**Time Commitment:** 2-4 months organizing; ongoing advocacy
**Legal Risk:** None (advocacy is protected; ordinances are legal)
**Impact Potential:** High (creates enforceable legal barriers)

- Prohibition on ICE cooperation or information sharing
- Healthcare access without deportation risk
- HIPAA protections extended (patient data not shared)
- Secure zones for vulnerable patients
- Precedent: Major hospitals (Mayo Clinic, University of Chicago) have sanctuary policies

**School Sanctuary**
- Prohibition on ICE enforcement on school property
- Right of children to attend school without fear
- Notification protocols for families
- Separation of school records from immigration enforcement
- Precedent: Many school districts have sanctuary policies

**Church/Religious Institution Sanctuary**
- Traditional sanctuary for persons in danger
- Coordination with immigration attorneys
- Temporary shelter and support
- Media engagement and public protection through visibility

**Campaign Actions:**
- Meet with institutional leadership (judges, hospital administrators, superintendents)
- Build internal coalition (judges, doctors, teachers, staff)
- External pressure (community organizations, media)
- Model policy development with legal analysis
- Implementation and monitoring

**Campaign Page Should Include:**

- Sample testimony for administrative proceedings

**Legal Basis:**

### 14. Continuity of Government Awareness

**Section 1: Constitutional Timeline**

- Election certification (varies by state, typically 5-6 weeks)
- Ballot recounts/challenges (30-day window per HAVA)
- State certification (mid-December)
- Electoral College vote (second Monday in December, historically)
- Congressional count of electoral votes (January 6)
- Inauguration Day (January 20, constitutional requirement)
- Each step has specific procedures, timelines, and participants

**Section 2: Legitimate vs. Illegitimate Actions**
- Legitimate: election challenges through courts, audits, recounts
- Illegitimate: preventing election certification, invalidating votes, blocking electoral college vote, preventing congressional count, refusing to transfer power after constitutional deadline

**Section 3: Historical Precedent**
- Peaceful transfers of power (norm since 1800)
- Contested elections (Hayes-Tilden 1876, Kennedy-Nixon 1960, Bush-Gore 2000)
- How each was resolved through constitutional process
- Outcomes when constitutional process fails (coups in other democracies)

**Section 4: Specific Crisis Scenarios**
- **Scenario A:** Disputed election results → constitutional resolution mechanisms
- **Scenario B:** Attempt to prevent state certification → legal action, public pressure
- **Scenario C:** Attempt to prevent electoral college vote → federal law protects electors
- **Scenario D:** Attempt to invalidate electoral college → Congress votes on challenges
- **Scenario E:** Refusal to transfer power after January 20 → constitutional violation, mass mobilization
- For each scenario: what triggers would justify mass resistance, how to respond

**Section 5: Mass Resistance Procedures**
- Pre-agreed triggers for general strike
- Communication networks and activation
- Demands to specific actors (Congress, military, courts)
- International pressure strategies
- Duration and escalation procedures
- Win conditions (resignation, elections, restoration of democracy)

**Campaign Actions:**
- Educational materials (short videos, infographics)
- Community education events
- Elected official briefings
- Media engagement
- Interactive scenario planning
- Legal expert interviews
- Historical case studies

**Campaign Page Should Include:**
- Constitutional timeline (interactive, with key documents linked)
- Legitimate vs. illegitimate action checklist
- Constitutional transfer requirements by office
- State election certification procedures
- Electoral college procedures and historical data
- Congressional procedures for counting electoral votes
- Key Supreme Court decisions on transfer of power
- 2016 and 2020 case studies (what happened, what could have happened)
- Video library: experts explaining procedures
- Scenario planning tool: "what if X happened, what would happen next?"
- Pre-written responses for specific scenarios
- General strike activation triggers and communication plan
- International precedent: how other democracies handled similar crises
- Legal analysis: what specific constitutional articles apply to each scenario

**Impact Metrics:**
- Educational materials viewed/shared
- Community education event attendance
